  2. All jobs. 8-week timeline from last Monday, one row per job, sorted by install. Tap a name to open the job.
  3. One job. Job picker, Next up, every date with provisional or confirmed, the shift buttons (−1 day, +1 day, +1 week), the tick list, rework on this job, and a History list from the change log.
  4. New job. One form: job number, brand, client name, suburb, scope summary, door finish, stone, site start, factory days (2), install days (3), notes. The proposed dates and any capacity clash appear under the form before you save.
  5. Rework. Raise in three taps and a line of text (job, cause, note). Open and closed lists, and the monthly tally of count, hours and dollars by cause.

Doers see the same screens with only their buttons live: tick a step, raise rework. Viewers see everything read-only. Phone, email and street address are never stored.

jobsJob number, brand, client name, suburb, scope summary, door finish (melamine or polyurethane), stone yes/no, site start, factory days, install days, status (Live, On hold, Complete, Cancelled), at risk, notes, date added.

decisionsEach "OK as is" on a Needs a decision item, saved with the dates it was judged on, so the item comes back if those dates change.
blocked_daysDate, factory only or everyone, reason. NSW public holidays 2026 to 2028 preloaded.
settingsCapacities and default durations, all editable.
change_logEvery add, change and delete on every table: who, when, the record before and after. Written by the database itself.

How it's built and kept safe

Node.js 22 with TypeScript, Express and Render Postgres. Pages are drawn on the server with a little plain JavaScript and no front-end framework, so there is little to keep upgraded. The date rules and the permission rules have tests that GitHub runs on every push.

Roles live in the database. Each request tells Postgres who is signed in, and a check on every table refuses any write that person's role can't make: a Doer can tick steps and raise rework and nothing else, a Viewer can write nothing. The same check writes the change log, so a bug in the app can't skip either.

Sign-in: type your email, get a link valid for 20 minutes, tap it, then tap Sign in. The second tap stops Outlook's link scanner from using the link up before you do. You stay signed in for 90 days on that phone. An address not on the list gets the same "check your email" screen and no email.

Backups: Render's own daily database backups, plus a copy at 2am Sydney time every night to a storage bucket outside Render, kept 90 days. I'll restore one into a test database before go-live to prove it works.

Loading the Notion data

A one-off import of the 18 live jobs (Active and Booked, excluding the ZZ rows) and their operations, frozen as a snapshot file in the repository. Notion's 12 operations fold into the hub's steps: Cut & edge, Assemble and QC & wrap become one Factory step; Material take-off and Deliver are dropped. Done stays done. Dates the team is already working to come in as confirmed; missing dates stay blank and land in Needs a decision, never invented. "Booked (pre-contract)" becomes Live. Door finish comes from the finish text (2-pac means polyurethane). The ZZ Factory internal dates become blocked days. Street addresses in notes are removed. Notion stays as it is until you switch over.
