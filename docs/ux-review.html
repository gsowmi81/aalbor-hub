Hub UX review

Every screen tested as every role, on a desktop and a phone, with and without script. What broke was fixed. Then a design and accessibility pass with the changes I'd make, from quick wins done today to three larger redesigns for you to decide on.

Bugs found and fixed today

  1. The "added" or "deleted" message vanished after a save that moved to another screen (adding a job, deleting one). The in-place save followed the redirect and ate the message. Now the new screen is swapped in with its message, and Back still works.
  2. The move box stayed open after choosing "Move everything after it" on the job page.
  3. Deleting a job with stock picked for it was refused by the ledger's never-edit rule, because clearing the job link counted as an edit. Migration 014.
  4. The Rework page scrolled sideways on a phone (the owners-only table pushed its column wide).
  5. A quantity over a million on a stock line crashed with a database error instead of a message.
  6. A form sent after the sign-in ran out showed a bare "Sign in first." Now it goes to the sign-in page with a plain message and returns to where you were.
  7. Smaller: the step tick reported an empty state to screen readers; "Show proposed dates" rewrote the whole page instead of refreshing the proposal.

Everything else held: roles are enforced by the database (a Doer's or Viewer's attempt is refused even when the form is forged), cross-site posts are refused, hostile text is escaped everywhere, oversized files get a message, double submits don't double-count, and every popover works by keyboard with a visible focus ring, Enter to open and Escape to close.

First impression

On a desktop the hub reads well. The Board is the strongest screen: one glance says what the week is, colour says which trade, orange says what's late. The Pipeline and the job timeline are clear and the copy is plain. The look is consistent and calm, which suits a tool people open forty times a day.

On a phone, before today, the story was different. The header (notice bar, company line, title, search, name, two rows of tabs) took the first 40% of the screen on every page, so Phil's first step was always below the fold. Three screens are very long: One job (about 14,000 pixels on a phone with a busy job), Settings (20,000), and Attention when flags pile up. Those three are where the real redesign effort should go. The phone header is fixed as of today: a short block and the tabs along the bottom, where thumbs are.

The hub's biggest UX risk is not any one screen. It is that an Editor's screens and a Doer's screens are the same screens with buttons removed. Phil doesn't need Pipeline, Jobs or the Board; he needs My day, the job in front of him, Flag and the bin QR. Christine needs the opposite. Redesign A below is about that.

The redesigns, in the order I'd do them

A · Home by role about 3 days

The tabs and the home screen depend on who is signed in. A Doer sees My day · This job · Stock · More, and "This job" is whatever they last opened or scanned. An Editor keeps Board · Pipeline · Attention · Jobs · More. Viewers get Board · Pipeline · Jobs. The bottom bar done today is the first half of this.

Why first: it is the difference between Phil opening the hub on his own and Phil being told to.

B · One job as sections about 2 days

A sticky strip of section tabs with counts under the job name. Steps opens by default; Materials, Files, Flags, Rework, Details and History are a tap away and the page never exceeds one or two screens. Links from Attention, the sheet and My day open the right section.

Why second: it is the screen everyone lands on, from every other screen.

C · Settings as pages about 1 day

A menu with one line of status per page, and the People page as cards. Only Editors go here, but they go here to fix the things that make the other screens wrong (areas, minimums, flag types), so it has to be quick.

D · Phone versions of the wide screens about 2 days

Pipeline, Need and supply and Count become card lists on phones. Same data, no sideways scrolling. The desktop tables stay.

E · Guidance that fades about 1 day

Each screen gets one short "How this works" card the first three times a person sees it, then a "?" in the corner. The hub has good explanatory lines today; they just show every time, so people stop reading them.

F · Undo and feedback about 1 day

Every tick, move and source shows a one-line confirmation at the bottom of the screen with Undo for ten seconds. The save message now sticks at the top on phones; a bottom bar with Undo is the finished version.

Together that is about ten days of build. A, B and C would be my first round; D, E and F the second, after a week of the team using the first. None of them changes the data or the rules, so the preview can keep running meanwhile.

For you to decide

  1. Keep the bottom tab bar on phones? It is live on the preview now. Try it on your phone for a day. If it feels wrong, it is one CSS block to remove.
  2. Role-shaped tabs (A). It means Phil cannot reach the Board from his phone without going through More. I think that is right; you may want the Board one tap away for Gus.
  3. What the Attention badge counts. Everything (today), only overdue, or only new since last look.
  4. The order of the rounds. A, B, C first is my recommendation; if the Monday meeting is the pain point, D (phone Pipeline) could move up.
