Jobman teardown and the hub's next step

What Jobman is made of, where the hub is clunky today, 19 recommendations, and a four-phase order to build them in. For George.

What this is built on. Jobman's own pages could not be opened from this session (jobman.com, docs.jobmanapp.com, academy.jobman.com and youtube.com are outside the session's allowed hosts), so the Jobman picture comes from search extracts of its help centre, academy, API docs and release notes up to v2.6.26 (5 Oct 2026). The video you sent is not in it. Allow those hosts, or tell me what the video shows, and I will fold it in. The hub findings come from running today's code with the 18 Notion jobs and screenshotting every screen at desktop and iPhone widths.

1. Short version

  • Jobman's planning is not cleverer than the hub's. Its scheduling engine is a target calendar of hours per labour centre, lead times backwards from install, and a dependency flag per task. The hub already has the equivalent in schedule.ts, with better rules for Aalbor's own flow.
  • Jobman feels more sophisticated for two reasons the hub lacks: every person has a screen shaped for them (the Kiosk gives Phil only his tasks), and status is derived from what the floor does, so managers read the system instead of feeding it.
  • The hub is clunky for one main reason: it is four long pages, each showing everything to everyone. Coming up on a phone is 33,759 pixels tall. One job is 14,607. Nothing is prioritised by who is looking.
  • Jobman's inventory model is the right blueprint for Phase 2, minus money: a catalogue with minimum and reorder quantities, a materials list per job imported from Cabinet Vision by code and quantity, "source from stock or add to the order list", a stock ledger, and a low-stock rule of on hand plus on order below minimum.
  • Do it in four phases: make it light (2 weeks), make it role-shaped and self-updating (3 weeks), stock (4 to 5 weeks), then scanning. The order matters because stock depends on a materials list per job, and that only works once the job screen is something Christine wants to open.

Two things about Jobman that are not features. It is an ERP, priced around $200 an account a month (third-party figure, unverified), and everything above only works once operations, labour centres, workflows and statuses are all set up and kept up. The hub's rules live in code and tests. That is its advantage, and the recommendations keep it.

3. Why the hub feels clunky today

Measured on the 18 imported jobs, signed in as George, at 1280 px wide and on an iPhone 13.

  1. Everything on one page. Coming up is tiles, the week board, 41 overdue items and 14 decisions, all expanded. On a phone it is 33,759 pixels tall, about 40 screens. The board, which is the point of the page, is the first 15%.
  2. The same page for everyone. Phil, Gus, Christine and George all land on the same board. Phil's three factory steps this week are mixed in with 60 other cards. Jobman's Kiosk shows a person their own tasks and nothing else.
  3. One job is a form, not a job. Thirteen steps appear twice: once as date inputs with Confirmed tick boxes, then again as the Checklist. Then tasks, rework, details and history. On a phone it is 14,607 pixels. Christine has to scroll past every date to tick one step done.
  4. A single date change is a round trip. Move… opens the Reschedule screen, then "Work it out", then "Save 1 change", then back. Three taps and two page loads for one date. The drag box on the board is quicker but only on a mouse or a wide screen.
  5. Overdue is a chore, not a signal. Forty-one overdue steps in the snapshot. When ticking lags, every other signal (capacity, needs a decision, at risk) is noise, and the Friday clean-up falls on Christine. Jobman avoids this by making the floor update status as a side effect of working.
  6. All jobs speaks in codes. CD, CM, DR, MI, F, P, PB, DL, IN, SM, SI, RW need a legend. The row for a job does not say what stage it is at.
  7. Every action reloads the page. A tick posts a form and re-renders the whole page; hub.js puts the scroll position back. On the factory wifi that is a visible pause per tick. It is not wrong, but it is why it feels old.
  8. Seven equal tabs. "One job" is a tab with a drop-down picker; you reach a job by choosing it from a list rather than searching or tapping it. Rework, Suggestions and Settings have the same weight as the board.
  9. Nothing reaches out. The hub waits to be looked at. The only email it sends is the sign-in link and one per bot batch. Jobman nudges with alerts and automatic status emails.

None of these are faults in the rules or the data. The database, the roles, the change log, dependencies, capacity and the Buildertrend suggestions are solid and should not be rebuilt. The step-change is in the screens and in where status comes from.

4. Recommendations

R1 to R8 are production planning. R9 to R12 are the floor. R13 to R19 are stock. Each says what Jobman does, what to build, and what changes under the hood.

Production planning

R1Split Coming up into Board and Attention

The board stays the home page and ends with the week. Overdue and Needs a decision move to an Attention screen, grouped by job (one line per job with a count, tap to expand), sorted by install date. The tiles link to it. The phone page drops from 40 screens to about 6.

Under the hood: views only. No migration.

R2My day: a screen per person

Jobman's Kiosk, without the clock. A Doer lands on My day: their steps for today and this week, in order, each with one big Done button, a note box and "Flag a problem" (R9). Gus sees check measures with suburb and the drawings link. Phil sees factory, delivery, doors back. Christine sees drafting, drawings and orders, plus the Attention count. George sees the board. Steps are matched by the owner already on each step (KINDS[kind].owner and steps.owner), so no new data is needed beyond a "works on" list per person in Settings.

Under the hood: people.areas (text array), a route /me, a per-role landing page.

R3One job as a timeline, not a form

One row per step: name, owner, date range, a status pill (Done, Confirmed, Provisional, Overdue), a Done tick, and the date itself as the tap target. Tapping a date opens a small box on the spot with the date picker and the three choices (move everything after, keep install, only this), the same box the drag already uses. Remove the separate Checklist panel; the Dates panel becomes it. "Save dates" and "Save and confirm all" stay for bulk edits, behind "Edit all dates".

Under the hood: views and hub.js. The quick reschedule route already exists.

R4Pipeline view

Jobman's rebuilt workflow board. Columns are the stages (Drafting, Check measure, Drawings, Materials, Factory, Painting, Install, Stone, Done); each live job is one card in the column of its first unticked step. The card shows days in this stage, days to install, and a red edge if anything on it is overdue. Cards move on their own as steps are ticked; nobody drags them. This is the Monday meeting screen: it answers "where is every job" in one glance, which the week board cannot.

Under the hood: a view over existing data. Stage = first step in FLOW not done.

R5Capacity by area in hours, with a 6-week load view

Jobman measures each labour centre in hours per day and shows available against booked. Replace the flat "2 kitchens a week" with four areas (Factory, Install crew, Check measures, Drafting), each with hours per working day and a list of who is in it. Each job's factory and install days become hours (days × hours per day, so today's numbers carry over), rework hours already count. The load view shows six weeks, one row per area, booked against available per day, coloured when over. Capacity adjustments replace blocked days: a date range, an area or a person, and a fraction (half day, one person off). Whole-factory closures stay as today.

Under the hood: labour_centres, capacity_adjustments, capacity() in hours; blocked_days kept as the all-closed case.

R6Pinned dates

Jobman's locked target date. A Pinned tick on a step (install, usually, once the client has the date) that no shift, squeeze or quick move changes without "Move anyway". Confirmed keeps meaning "matches Buildertrend"; Pinned means "promised".

Under the hood: steps.pinned, honoured in reschedule(), shown as a padlock.

R7Propose the first week with room

Jobman does not do this; it is a hub advantage to keep. When a new job is proposed, show the factory and install weeks it would land in with their load from R5, and offer "next week with room" as a one-tap alternative. Needs a decision already catches overbooking after the fact; this catches it at entry.

R8Fewer tabs, and a job search

Board · Pipeline · My day · Jobs (the Gantt, with a stage column and full step names in a hover title) · Attention · More (Rework, Suggestions, Stock, Settings). A search box in the header finds a job by number, client or suburb and opens it. Drop the One job tab.

The floor

R9Flag a problem

Jobman's alert. From My day or any step: a type (materials short, drawing query, site not ready, damaged, other), one line, an optional photo from the phone camera. It lands on Attention for Christine and George with the step and the person, and can be turned into a rework or cleared with a note. Rework stays for work that has to be done again; a flag is for "I'm stuck".

Under the hood: issues table with hub_guard and hub_log; Doers may add; photos in Render disk or R2, 1 MB cap.

R10Partial updates without a page reload

A tick, a flag or a date move should change the row, not the page. Keep the server-rendered forms (they work without script) and add one small layer in hub.js: on submit, post the form, receive the re-rendered panel, swap it in. No framework; the rule in CLAUDE.md stands. This removes most of the "clunk" by itself.

Under the hood: routes answer with the panel's HTML when the request asks for it (Accept: text/html-fragment), full page otherwise.

R11Nudges by email, then a daily digest

Resend is already wired. Three emails: a Monday 7 am "your week" per person from My day; a daily 7 am note to the owner of any step that went overdue yesterday (not to Christine); and a flag notification to Editors. Each has one link to the step. Push notifications on phones can come later if email proves too slow.

Under the hood: a Render cron like the backup, people.email_digest on or off.

R12Job sheet with a QR code

The cheap half of Jobman's Deliverables. A printable one-page job sheet (number, client, suburb, finish, dates, cabinet count) with a QR code that opens My day for that job on a phone. Stuck on the trolley, it means Phil ticks factory done and delivery done from the trolley, not from a list. Scanning cabinets one by one (Jobman's checkpoints) is Phase D if the sheet proves useful.

Stock (Phase 2)

This is Jobman's inventory design with the money removed. The brief's section 6 maps onto it almost line for line.

R13Catalogue

Load the 183 Notion lines. Each item: code (the Cabinet Vision product code, which is what makes R14 automatic), name, kind (board always stocked, board to order, standard hardware, per-job hardware, consumable), unit (sheet, box, each, metre), supplier, supplier lead days, minimum, reorder quantity, bin label, active. Jobman will only auto-flag low stock when supplier, SKU and minimum are all set; the hub's edit form should say so on the row.

R14Materials per job, from Cabinet Vision by file

Jobman's JMSync sends only quantity and product code, keyed by job number. Do the same without the Windows app: Christine exports the CV material report for the job (it carries codes and quantities; sheet counts are right once the job is nested) and drops the file on the job's Materials panel. Unknown codes are listed for a one-time match to a catalogue item, remembered after that. Hand entry stays for one-offs. Later, a small reader script for the optimiser database can replace the export.

Under the hood: job_materials (job, item, quantity, source: stock / order / none), code_aliases.

R15Demand against supply, by factory date

Six weeks out, per board colour and per hardware line: needed (jobs by factory date), on hand, on order with expected date, and the gap. Each shortfall carries an "order by" date (factory date minus supplier lead days, in working days). This is the "Jones needs 6 Satin White on 3 Nov, you have 2, order 4 by 29 Oct" line from the brief, and it is where stock pays for itself.

R16The Monday order list

Jobman's Pending Items, stopping before the purchase order. One list grouped by supplier: shortfalls from R15, per-job hardware not in stock, standard items flagged low (R18). Angela ticks Ordered and types the expected date; the item moves to on order; receiving (the factory sign-in) ticks In. Nothing on hand or on order can be listed twice. No order numbers, no prices.

Under the hood: orders (item, quantity, job or stock, ordered on, expected, received on).

R17Stock ledger, counts and offcuts

On hand is never typed; it is the sum of movements: received, picked for a job (when factory starts, from the job's materials), counted (Phil's Friday count sets a new figure and logs the difference), adjusted with a reason, offcut logged. Phil's Friday screen lists the stocked boards with the last count and a number box each; the quarterly stocktake is the same screen for every active item. Offcuts of non-stocked colours over half a sheet are logged with colour, size and location and shown on R15 when that colour is next needed.

R18Running low, from the bin

Two-bin for the standard range. Each bin label carries a QR code; scanning it on a phone opens one button, "Running low", which puts the item on the order list with who and when. The same page shows what's on order already, so nobody flags twice.

R19Materials readiness on the job

Once R14 to R16 exist, "Materials in" stops being a tick someone remembers. The job shows ready / short (with the lines short and their expected dates), and a job whose factory date is inside a shortfall shows up on Attention and on the pipeline card. This is the single biggest planning gain from stock, and it is why stock comes after the planning screens, not alongside them.

5. Phasing

Why not everything at once: the team is two to three weeks into Phase 1, and the brief's success test (board current every Monday, Overdue cleared each Friday) has not been run yet. The screen changes need no new data and get adoption; the floor changes need the screens; stock needs a materials list per job, which needs a job screen Christine opens willingly and a Cabinet Vision export habit. Each phase ends with a week of use before the next starts. Weeks are build effort, not calendar time.

Phase A: make it light (about 2 weeks)

R1 Board and Attention · R3 One job as a timeline · R8 tabs and search · R10 partial updates · R6 Pinned dates.

One migration (pinned). What you notice: the phone pages are short, a tick or a date move happens where you are, and you find a job by typing its number.

Phase B: role-shaped and self-updating (about 3 weeks)

R2 My day · R4 Pipeline · R9 Flag a problem · R11 nudges · R5 capacity in hours with the load view · R7 first week with room · R12 job sheet with QR.

Migrations: people areas, issues, labour centres and adjustments. What you notice: Phil and Gus open the hub on their own because it shows only their work; the Monday meeting runs off Pipeline; overdue nudges go to the person, not to Christine; capacity says hours and names.

Phase C: stock (about 4 to 5 weeks)

R13 catalogue (week 1) · R14 materials per job and the CV upload (week 2) · R17 ledger and Phil's count (week 2 to 3) · R15 demand against supply and R16 the order list (week 3 to 4) · R18 bins · R19 readiness on the job (week 5).

Start with the catalogue and Phil's first count, so there is a true on-hand figure before any demand is shown. The order list goes live only when four weeks of jobs have materials loaded, or the shortfalls are fiction.

Phase D: later, if the job sheet earns it

Cabinet-level labels and scanning at assembled, loaded, delivered, installed (Jobman's Deliverables). A reader for the Cabinet Vision optimiser database. Two-way Buildertrend. Push notifications.

6. What not to copy from Jobman

  • Timesheets as the engine. Jobman's status, bar charts and reports all hang off time logged against tasks, and its own guidance is that kiosk users should start and stop tasks but not complete them. Aalbor said no timesheets. A Done tick and a flag are enough signal, and they take one tap.
  • Configurable workflows, operations and statuses. They are how Jobman serves every manufacturer; they are also why it needs an administrator course. The hub's flow is Aalbor's flow, in code, with tests.
  • The hour-by-hour target calendar with split segments. Right for a machine shop; too fine for two kitchens a week. Day-level dates with hours for capacity is the right grain.
  • Purchase orders, supplier pricing and the catalogue integrations. Out of scope, permanently (brief, section 1).
  • A desktop sync app. A file upload does the same job for one drafter.

7. Decisions I need from you

  1. Phase A first, as above, or Phase B's My day first? My day is the bigger step-change for Phil and Gus; Phase A is the bigger one for you and Christine.
  2. Capacity in hours per area (R5), or keep kitchens per week and only add the load view? Hours makes rework and part-days count properly; kitchens is what the team says out loud.
  3. Who is in each area for My day: Phil and the factory sign-in in Factory; Gus in Check measures; Christine in Drafting; install crew (Sione?) as a person or a shared sign-in?
  4. Can Christine export the Cabinet Vision material report per job as a file today, and which report? That decides whether R14 is an upload or a typed list in the first cut.
  5. Nudges by email: yes for everyone, or Editors only to start?
  6. The video. Which hosts to allow, or what in it you want matched.
