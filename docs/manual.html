Aalbor Kitchens · Production Hub

The Aalbor Hub manual

The hub is where Aalbor Kitchens plans production and keeps stock. It replaces Jobman for those two jobs and nothing else: quotes, invoices, purchase orders and client details stay in Buildertrend, Xero and Cabinet Vision.

This manual is in three parts. Your week in the hub is one page per kind of person: what you open, when, and what you do there. The screens is the reference, one page per screen. How things are done follows a job, a rework, a flag or an order across screens.

It lives in the hub itself (More → Manual, or the "?" on a screen) and changes with the hub, so it is never older than what you are looking at. Dates in it are examples.

If something on a screen doesn't match this manual, the manual is wrong. Tell George or Christine.

Your week in the hub

The owner's week

George and Gus. You see everything and can change everything. You are also the only two who see who was responsible for a rework.

Every day, two minutes

  1. Open Attention. The number on the tab is how many things need a human: flags, overdue steps, dates that break a rule, weeks that are overbooked, jobs short of materials. Work the list top to bottom; each item has its answer next to it (OK as is, Change dates, New date, Fixed).
  2. Glance at the Board for the week. Faded cards are provisional dates nobody has confirmed yet. Red cards are rework.

Monday

  • The Monday email lists everyone's week and the installs finished last week with their photos. Read it before the production meeting.
  • Pipeline is the meeting screen: every live job in one row, soonest install first, the dark outline on the stage each job is at.
  • Load shows the next six weeks of factory and install hours booked against hours available, day by day. A red day is overbooked.

When a site manager signs off an install

Gus's My day shows Needs your sign-off when an installer has marked a job finished from their side. Inspect, then tap Done: gaps adjusted? site left clean? and your photos. That tick is the official completion; the installer's button is not.

Monthly

  • Rework has the monthly tally and, for owners only, Who was responsible. Record it on each rework as it closes; the tally is only as good as that.
  • Check Settings → People: who has signed in, who has no areas set (the menu says so), installers who should be switched off.

Only owners can

  • Make someone an owner, or change an owner's record.
  • See and record who was responsible for a rework.
  • Make and revoke the Buildertrend bot's API tokens (Settings → Buildertrend bot).

The office's week (Christine)

You are the hub's editor. New jobs, every date, materials and the order list, the bot's suggestions and the settings are yours. Most of your week is the Board, Attention and the Suggestions screen.

Every morning

  1. Attention. Overdue steps and dates that break a rule, grouped by job, soonest install first. For an overdue step: New date opens the Reschedule screen with the rest of the job following. For a rule it can't keep: OK as is if the dates are right anyway, Change dates otherwise.
  2. Suggestions (More → Suggestions; the count is on the More tab). The Buildertrend bot posts what it found overnight: a date that doesn't match, a new job, a status change. Accept applies it and ticks the date Confirmed; Decline leaves the hub as it is; Buildertrend is wrong records that Buildertrend needs fixing, not the hub.
  3. The Board for anything that moved. Drag a card sideways to move it to another day; the box asks what happens to the rest of the job.

When a job is sold

More → New job: job type, job number, client and suburb, and the one date you know (site start, or the install if that is what the client has been promised). Show proposed dates fills every other date from the job type's rules and shows which factory and install weeks the job lands in and how full they are. Save. Every date is provisional until you confirm it. See A job from start to finish.

When plans arrive

On the job, Files → Add a file: contract plans (what the client signed) or production plans (signed off, build from these). A new file of the same class becomes the current version; the old one stays as superseded. The job sheet and the installer's site page show the current ones.

Materials for a job

Once the production plans are in, on the job's Materials section load the Cabinet Vision cut list (or type the lines), then Source: the hub takes free stock first, then stock already on order, and lists the rest on the order list by the date it must be ordered. See Materials for a job.

Monday: the order list

More → Stock → Order list is grouped by supplier. Ordered with the expected date when you place it; In when it arrives (a partial stays on order). Need and supply is the six-week view behind it. See The weekly stock cycle.

Ticks you own

Materials ordered, on each job, once the order is placed. Confirmed, on each date, once it matches Buildertrend (moving a date confirms it for you).

Settings you keep

Blocked days (public holidays are loaded to 2028; add shutdowns and clean-outs), the office message on My day and the Board, flag types and who looks after them, installer links on each job.

The factory's week (Phil and the factory sign-in)

You tick things done, count stock, pick materials, log offcuts and flag problems. You don't move dates: if a date is wrong, flag it and the office moves it.

Every day

Open the hub on the phone or the factory PC. It opens on My day: your steps overdue, today, later this week and next week, one Done button each. When a step is finished, tap Done. The office sees it straight away. If you ticked the wrong one, Undo is on the screen for ten seconds; after that, open the job and untick it.

Your steps are the ones in your areas (Materials in, Factory, and Delivery sits with the factory). The shared factory sign-in ticks Materials in, Doors back from painting and Delivery.

The TV on the wall

The Factory TV shows this week and next: materials in, cut, painting, delivery and installs, by day. It refreshes itself. If it's blank, the link needs remaking in Settings; ask the office.

Before cutting a job

Open the job (This job, or scan the job sheet's QR code). Materials shows whether everything is on hand. Pick for the factory takes the lines off the shelf in the hub as you take them off the shelf in the factory. Check Offcuts first for colours we don't keep.

When something is wrong

Flag on the step, or Flag a problem on the job: pick what's wrong, one line, photos. It goes by email to whoever looks after that kind of problem and sits on Attention until it's fixed. Materials short, a drawing query, something damaged, a machine problem.

When a job needs rework

More → Rework → Raise rework: the job, the cause, one line on what happened, and tick what needs doing (drawings, factory work with hours, delivery, install). The office dates it.

Friday: the count

More → Stock → Count. The standing lines, with what the hub thinks is on the shelf. Type what you see; leave a box empty to skip it. Save the count. Each difference is logged with your name.

A bin running low

Scan the bin's QR label and tap Running low. That puts the reorder quantity on the order list once; a second scan tells you it's already there.

Offcuts

More → Stock → Offcuts → Log an offcut: the colour, size and where it is. Used when it's gone.

The site manager's week

You check measure, keep an eye on installs, raise rework for site problems and sign off each install. Gus runs the site teams.

Every day

The hub opens on My day: your check measures and installs overdue, today, this week and next, one Done button each. Flags for you are the problems you look after, three at a time, the rest on Attention.

Check measures

Tick Done on the check measure when it's done. Add a note if the measure changed anything. If it changes the dates, flag it (drawing query) so the office moves them.

When an installer says they're finished

Needs your sign-off at the top of My day lists installs the installer has marked finished from their side, with a link to their log and photos. Inspect. Then tap Done: gaps adjusted? site left clean? and your photos. That is the official completion; nothing else is.

Something wrong on site

Open the job (This job, or the job sheet's QR) and Flag a problem with photos, or raise rework: More → Rework → Raise rework, pick the job and the cause, tick what needs doing. The office dates it; it appears in red on the Board.

The installer's link

Each job has a Site section with the installer's daily log, photos and anything they reported missing. The office makes and stops the links; ask Christine for one when a crew starts.

Installers

You don't sign in. The office sends you a link for the job. It opens the job's site page on your phone and nothing else. Keep the link; it works until the office stops it.

What's on the page

  • Plans: production plans are the ones to build from; contract plans are what the client signed.
  • Install: the dates.
  • Today's log: what happened today, with photos. Do this every day you're on site.
  • Something missing or wrong?: one line and photos. It goes straight to the office and the site manager.
  • Finished from your side: tap it when the install is done as far as you're concerned, with the finished photos. It is not the sign-off. A site manager inspects and signs it off; the page then says Signed off.
  • The log: everything you've written, newest first, and whether each missing item has been sorted.

If the link stops

The job is finished, or the office has stopped it. Ask the office for a new one.

Viewers (Angela and Michael)

You see the Board, Pipeline and every job, and can't change anything. The tabs are Board · Pipeline · Jobs · More.

  • Board: this week and next, one card per step.
  • Pipeline: every live job in one row, where each is up to.
  • Jobs: every live job on one chart.
  • Find a job in the header: a number, a client's name or a suburb.
  • More → Spreadsheet downloads every job, step and rework.

If you need to tick, flag or move something, ask George or Christine to change your role.

The screens

Board

The week, one card per step, in rows by area: Drafting for contract, Check measure, Production drawings, Materials, Factory (cut, painting, delivery), Install, Stone, Rework. Day columns are working days: Monday to Friday, with Saturday when Settings → Capacity says Saturdays are working days. Sundays, public holidays and blocked days aren't shown.

Reading it

  • A faded, dashed card is a provisional date: proposed by the hub, not yet confirmed against Buildertrend.
  • A red card is rework.
  • A card with a line through it is done.
  • The top tiles count this week: overdue, provisional, rework and the week's load.
  • The office message (Settings → Message) sits at the top.
  • This week · Next week · Today switch the week. All · Aalbor · Origin filter by brand.

Doing things

  • Tap a card to open the job on that step.
  • Editors: drag a card sideways to move it to another day. A box asks what happens to the rest of the job: Move everything after it (every later step moves by the same working days), Keep the install date (only the steps between move, as little as the rules allow) or Only this one. See every date first opens the Reschedule screen instead. The move saves at once and confirms the dates it changed. If it would put a step before something it waits for, nothing saves and the Reschedule screen opens with the clash.
  • On a phone the board is a day list; each card has Move….
  • The Suggestions panel shows what the bot is waiting on.

Pipeline

Every live job, one row each, soonest install first, one cell per stage: Drafting, Check measure, Drawings, Materials, Factory, Painting, Delivery, Install, Stone, and open rework.

Reading a cell

CellMeans
Green, ✓Done
AmberOn this week
Grey with a dateLater
RedOverdue: the date has passed and nobody ticked it
Dashed, ?No date
–Skipped: undated but a later stage is done (the imported jobs never had Drafting ticked)

The dark outline is the stage the job is at: the first one not ticked done. The dot on the Materials cell is readiness: green ready to cut, amber on order, red short or on order for after the factory date. The count under each column heading is how many jobs are at that stage.

Tap a cell to open that step on the job. On a phone each job is a card with its stages as chips.

Attention

Everything that needs a human, grouped by job, soonest install first. The number on the tab is the count. Doers see it under More.

In order

  1. Flagged problems: open flags, who looks after each, Looking at it / Fixed (with a note) / Raise rework for this.
  2. Short for the factory: jobs whose materials are short, or on order for after the factory date.
  3. Per job: overdue steps (New date opens the Reschedule screen; Done if it was done and nobody ticked it) and decisions.
  4. Capacity: weeks and days with more hours booked than available.

Needs a decision

A rule the dates don't keep: a step before the one it waits for, a pinned date clashed with, an install without a site start, an overbooked week. Each says what the hub would do about it.

  • OK as is records that you looked and the dates are right. The item stays away until those dates change.
  • Change dates opens the dates it's about.

Each group folds. Tap the job's name to open it.

My day

Your steps this week, one Done button each. Doers land here when they sign in. "Yours" means a step in one of your areas (set under Settings → People) or with your first name on it.

Sections, top to bottom

  • Needs your sign-off (site managers): installs an installer has marked finished from their side, with their log and photos. Done here is the official sign-off.
  • Overdue, Today, Flags for you (three, the rest on Attention), Later this week, Next week.

Done

Tap Done. The step leaves the list and the office sees it. For ten seconds a line at the top says what you ticked with Undo. Install cabinets asks two questions first (gaps adjusted? site left clean?) and takes up to three photos.

Flag

Flag under a step opens a short form: what's wrong, one line, photos. See Flags.

From the job sheet

Scanning the QR code on a job sheet opens that job's open steps, whoever's they are, each with Done and Flag. The plans are linked at the top.

If it says your areas aren't set

You only see steps with your name on them. Ask George or Christine to tick your areas under Settings → People.

One job

Everything about one job. The strip under the name is the sections; one shows at a time. The address keeps the section, so a link from Attention or My day lands on the right one.

The top

The job number and site start, the name, the scope, and pills: door finish, painting, stone, brand, anything needing a decision, Materials readiness, the current plans, Drawings folder ↗ and Job sheet ↗. Next up is the next step with a date.

Steps

Every standard step in order: tick, name, owner, date, pills (Done, Overdue, Confirmed or Provisional, Pinned, Installer finished).

  • Tick a step when it's done (Doers and Editors). Install cabinets asks about gaps and the site and takes photos.
  • Tap the date to move it: a box with a date picker and the three choices (everything after it, keep the install date, only this one). Saves at once, confirmed. Editors.
  • Pin a promised date: nothing moves it until it's unpinned, and a clash with it needs a decision.
  • Flag a problem on that step.
  • Edit all dates changes several at once; tick Confirmed as each matches Buildertrend. Save and confirm all does both.
  • Move the whole job: −1 day, +1 day, +1 week for every open step; Work out the provisional dates again redoes the unconfirmed ones from site start.

Materials

The job's lines and where each stands. Source, Pick for the factory, Add a line, Load the Cabinet Vision cut list. See Materials for a job.

Files

Contract plans, production plans and other files, versioned. Add a file (Editors). The current of each class is linked at the top of the job, on the job sheet and on the installer's site page. Earlier versions fold away as superseded.

Flags

Open and fixed flags on the job. Flag a problem.

Site

The installer's log and photos, anything reported missing, and (Editors) the installer links: Make a link for an installer, Stop. When the installer has marked the install finished, a note says a site manager needs to sign it off.

Tasks (Editors)

Extra work just this job needs ("Install painted doors"): a name, an area, who, dates. It shows in its area's row on the Board and moves with that area's step.

Rework

Rework on this job, each with its tasks. Raise rework, Set dates, Add a rework item, Close this rework (with the rough extra hours and material cost). Owners also record who was responsible.

Details (Editors) · Notes (everyone else)

Job number, client, suburb, brand, finish, painting, stone, days, scope, notes, status (live, on hold, complete, cancelled), the drawings folder link. Delete this job is here, for a job added by mistake only.

History

Every change on this job: who, when, before and after. Changes the bot's suggestions made say "(suggestion #n)".

Jobs (the chart)

Every live job from this week on, one line each, sorted by install, with every dated step as a bar. Orange means a date has passed and nobody ticked it. Week headings and job names stay put while you scroll.

  • Tap a job name to open it.
  • Tap a bar to pick a date for it on the Reschedule screen.
  • Editors, with a mouse: drag a bar to move it. The same box as the Board asks what happens to the rest of the job, then saves, confirmed.

Rework

More → Rework. Raising, dating and closing rework, and the monthly tally.

Raise rework

The job, the cause (drafting, measure, factory, delivery, install, stone, supplier, client, other), one line on what happened, and tick what needs doing: drawings, factory work (hours), delivery, install (hours). Doers' rework comes in undated; an Editor's start date fills the dates in order and confirms them. Each task is a red card in its area's row on the Board.

Open rework

Each with its tasks and dates. Editors: Set dates, Edit (cause, what happened, each task's name, who, row, hours), Add a rework item (a name, a category, hours, dates), Close this rework with the rough extra hours and material cost. Owners: Record who was responsible.

Monthly tally

Rework raised per month by cause. Owners also see the tally by person responsible.

See Rework, start to finish.

Load

More → Load. Six weeks of hours booked against hours available, per day, for the Factory and the Install crew.

Hours available come from Settings → Capacity (hours a day per weekday, less any leave or shutdown). Hours booked: a kitchen's factory or install day books the hours in "Hours in a factory or install day" (Settings → Durations); a rework task books its own hours. A red day is overbooked; the jobs on it are listed. Weekends, holidays and blocked days are 0.

Use it to see where a new job fits; New job shows the same for the weeks it lands in.

Suggestions

More → Suggestions. The Buildertrend bot checks Buildertrend each weekday morning and posts what doesn't match the hub: a date, a status, a note, a job the hub doesn't have. Nothing changes until an Editor decides. The count waiting is on the More tab.

Deciding (Editors)

Each suggestion shows the hub's value and the proposed one, grouped by job, urgent first.

  • Accept applies it. A date is ticked Confirmed. If the hub has changed since the bot looked, it's marked "Couldn't apply" and nothing changes; look at the job and decide afresh.
  • Decline: the hub is right as it is.
  • Buildertrend is wrong: the hub is right and Buildertrend needs fixing. Recorded so the bot stops suggesting it.
  • Tick several and Accept selected or Decline selected.
  • Supersede all … waiting clears the queue before the bot rebuilds it.

A new job from the bot keeps only the fields the hub holds: the suburb, never the street address, phone or email.

Decided suggestions stay on the screen under their status; none are deleted. Changes made by accepting show in the job's History as "(suggestion #n)".

The email address the bot's batches go to, and the API tokens it signs in with, are under Settings → Buildertrend bot.

New job

More → New job (Editors).

  1. Job type: polyurethane painted doors or melamine doors. It sets the finish, painting, stone, factory and install days and the weeks from site start to install. Change any of them on the job afterwards.
  2. Job number from Buildertrend. Typing it fills the site start from the number (270301 is 1 Mar 2027); change the date if that isn't right.
  3. Client, suburb, brand, scope.
  4. The one date you know: site start, check measure, drawings start or install start. The hub works every other date from it; a blank site start is worked out backwards.
  5. Show proposed dates. The proposal lists every step and where the job lands: the factory and install weeks with hours booked before and after it against hours available. If the week is full, Use the next week with room moves the date you worked from and shows the new proposal, without saving.
  6. Save. Every date is provisional until it's confirmed.

A job can also arrive from the bot as a suggestion; accepting it does the same with the dates Buildertrend has.

Settings

More → Settings (Editors). A menu of pages, each with a line saying where things stand; a warning in orange means something is set wrong (people with no areas, a flag type with nobody responsible, no Factory TV link).

People

Hours a day per weekday for the Factory and the Install crew (Saturday too when it's on), and periods with different hours (a shutdown is 0, someone on leave is less, an extra pair of hands is more).

Stock: Need and supply

More → Stock. The first screen is the six-week view: what jobs need, by the week of their factory date, against what we have.

The tiles

Short (lines the next six weeks need and we don't have), Running low (standing lines at or under their minimum with nothing on order), Jobs short (can't cut yet), To order (on the order list).

Need against supply

One row per item: the quantity needed each of the next six weeks, on hand, spoken for (allocated to jobs and not yet picked), free, on order and when it's due, the gap, and by when to order it (the factory date less the supplier's lead days). Put the gaps on the order list adds every gap as a line. "Late" means the order-by date has passed.

Running low

The standing lines at or under minimum. Add to the list puts the reorder quantity on the order list.

Jobs short for the factory

Live jobs with materials loaded that aren't ready: which lines and why. Open goes to the job's Materials.

Covered

The lines jobs need that are on hand or on order, folded away.

On hand is always the sum of the ledger: opening balances from the July stocktake, then every count, receipt, pick and offcut. Nobody types an on-hand figure.

Stock: Items

More → Stock → Items. The catalogue: name, code, unit, supplier, category, standing (always kept) or bought to order, minimum and reorder quantity, bin, lead days.

  • Search by name, code, supplier or bin; filter by kind or standing lines only.
  • Add an item, Edit the item, Import or update from a spreadsheet (a CSV matched by code or name), Bin labels ↗ (QR labels for the standing lines).
  • An item's page: on hand, spoken for, free, on order; the ledger (every move, newest first); Record the count and Move stock (a correcting move, with a reason); its orders, offcuts, other codes the cut list uses for it, and the jobs that need it.

Editors and anyone with the Stock tick change items. Doers count and move.

Stock: Order list

More → Stock → Order list. Open lines grouped by supplier, each saying what it's for (a job, running low, a gap).

  • Ordered: when the order is placed, with the expected date. The line goes amber and the jobs waiting on it show "on order".
  • In: when it arrives, with the quantity. A partial stays on order for the rest. Stock received for a job marks that job's line as on hand.
  • Drop: a line that shouldn't be ordered.
  • Add a line for anything else.
  • Received and dropped folds away at the bottom.

Christine runs the list. The factory can mark lines In as deliveries arrive.

Stock: Bins

How things are done

Signing in

Go to the hub's address and type your email. If it's on the list, a link arrives within a minute (check junk mail). The link works for twenty minutes and once; after that, ask for another. You stay signed in on that phone or PC for weeks. Sign out is in the header.

Only addresses under Settings → People can sign in. Five links in twenty minutes is the limit; wait and use the newest.

Installers don't sign in; they get a link to one job. The factory TV opens with its own link.

Add the hub to your phone's home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen on an iPhone, the browser menu on Android); it then opens like an app.

A job from start to finish

  1. Sold. Christine adds it (More → New job) with the job type, number and the one date known. Every date is provisional. Or the bot suggests it from Buildertrend and she accepts.
  2. Plans. Contract plans go on the job's Files. Drafting for contract is ticked when the drafting is done.
  3. Check measure. The site manager ticks it on My day. Anything the measure changes is flagged as a drawing query.
  4. Production drawings. Christine ticks it when the production plans are signed off and adds them to Files; they become the current plans everywhere.
  5. Materials. The cut list is loaded on the job's Materials and sourced; what's short goes on the order list by its order-by date. Christine ticks Materials ordered once the order is placed. The factory ticks Materials in when it lands; the factory email goes out.
  6. Confirmed dates. As each date matches Buildertrend, it's ticked Confirmed (moving a date confirms it). The bot flags any that don't match.
  7. Factory. The factory picks the materials, cuts, edges and assembles, and ticks Cut, edge and assemble. Polyurethane doors go to the painter and come back: Doors to painting, Doors back from painting.
  8. Delivery. The factory ticks it.
  9. Install. The installer logs each day on the site page and marks it finished from their side. The site manager inspects and ticks Install cabinets done with the two answers and photos: that is the completion.
  10. Stone. Stone measure, then stone install, each the next working day or later.
  11. Complete. Christine sets the status to Complete on the job's Details. It leaves the Board and Pipeline; History keeps everything.

Where a date slips, see Moving dates. Where something goes wrong, Flags and Rework.

Moving dates

Working days are Monday to Friday, and Saturday when the switch under Settings → Capacity is on; never Sundays, public holidays or blocked days. Dates depend on each other: check measure → drawings → materials ordered (soft) → materials in → factory → painting → delivery → install → stone measure → stone install. A move that breaks a hard rule needs a decision. Done steps never move. Pinned dates never move.

Three ways to move one date

  • On the Board, drag the card to another day (Editors, desktop). On a phone, Move… on the card.
  • On the job, tap the date.
  • On Jobs, drag the bar with a mouse, or tap it.

Each asks the same question: what happens to the rest of the job?

  • Move everything after it: every later step moves by the same number of working days. The usual answer when the job has slipped.
  • Keep the install date: the steps between this one and the install move only as far as the rules force, using spare days first. For when the client has been promised the install.
  • Only this one: nothing else moves. For a date that was simply wrong.
  • See every date first opens the Reschedule screen with every date editable before anything saves.

The move saves at once and confirms the dates it changed. If it would put a step before the one it waits for, nothing saves and the Reschedule screen opens with the clash; Move anyway if you mean it.

If someone else moved the step while your page was open, the move is refused and says so; reload and look again.

The whole job

On the job, Move the whole job: −1 day, +1 day, +1 week for every open step. Rework and pinned dates stay.

A promised date

Pin it on the job. Nothing moves it, and anything that clashes with it shows under Needs a decision.

Rework, start to finish

  1. Raise it. More → Rework, or Raise rework on the job, or Raise rework for this on a flag. The job, the cause, one line on what happened, and tick what needs doing: drawings, factory work (hours), delivery, install (hours). Anyone who can tick can raise it.
  2. Date it. Editors: a start date on the raise form, or Set dates on the rework, fills the dates in order (drawings, then factory work for the hours, then delivery, then install) and confirms them. Each task is a red card in its area's row on the Board and on My day for whoever does it; the factory hours count against capacity.
  3. Do it. Tick each task done as it's finished, like any step.
  4. Close it. Close this rework with the rough extra hours and material cost. Owners record who was responsible.

Moving one task moves the later ones of the same rework by the same working days; the main job never moves because of rework. The monthly tally on the Rework page is by cause.

Flags

A flag is a problem someone needs to deal with: materials short or wrong, a drawing query, site not ready, something damaged, a machine problem, missing on site, something else. Each kind has a person who looks after it (Settings → Flagged problems).

Raising one. Flag under a step on My day or the job, or Flag a problem on the job: pick the kind, one line, photos. It goes by email to the person responsible and the Editors, shows first on Attention and in the tab's count, on the job's Flags section, and under Flags for you on My day for the person responsible.

Dealing with it. Looking at it, then Fixed with a note. Whoever raised it, the person responsible, or an Editor. Raise rework for this opens the rework form with the flag's note.

An installer's "something missing" on the site page is a flag of the kind Missing on site.

Materials for a job

On the job's Materials section.

  1. Load the lines. Load the Cabinet Vision cut list (a CSV: the code in the first column, the quantity in the last number column) or Add a line by hand. Codes the hub doesn't know go to a one-time match page; the match is remembered. Tick "From the nested file" when the sheet counts are the real ones.
  2. Source. The hub takes free stock first (leaving what jobs with an earlier factory date still need, unless unticked), then stock already on order (that much of the order becomes this job's), and lists the rest on the order list for this job, to be ordered by the factory date less the supplier's lead days.
  3. Watch the pill. The job's top shows Materials: ready to cut, on order (with the expected date, and a warning if that's after the factory date), or short. The same shows on the Pipeline's Materials cell and under Short for the factory on Attention and Need and supply.
  4. Pick. When the factory starts, Pick for the factory takes the allocated lines off the shelf as picked moves.

Lines can be removed and reloaded; the same code loaded again replaces the line.

The weekly stock cycle

  • Daily: deliveries are marked In on the order list as they land (quantity; a partial stays on order). The factory picks as jobs start, logs offcuts, scans a bin when it's running low.
  • Friday: the factory counts the standing lines (Stock → Count). Each difference is logged.
  • Monday: Christine opens Need and supply: the gaps for the next six weeks and by when to order each, then Order list grouped by supplier, places the orders and marks them Ordered with the expected dates.
  • As jobs get production plans: their cut lists are loaded and sourced, so the six-week view is real.

Minimums, reorder quantities, lead days and bins live on each item and were empty at the start; set them as you go.
